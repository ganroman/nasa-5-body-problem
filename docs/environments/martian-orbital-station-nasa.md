# Martian orbital habitat: functions, logistics, and crew

Research reference for **Flame in Freefall** · Team Five-Body Problem · 14 November 2026

A habitat in Mars orbit could support crew operations, scientific work, transfers to the surface, and preparation for the journey home. It would be a microgravity environment unless specifically designed to create artificial gravity. It is distinct from a base on the Martian surface.

## Status and interpretation

There is no inhabited NASA station orbiting Mars. NASA has studied a **Mars Transit Habitat** that carries astronauts between Earth and Mars and remains in orbit during part of the expedition. This does not require a separate permanent station. Published concepts are dated studies, not a finalized deployment plan. [NASA habitation development research](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20230008144)

The breakdown below is a **proposed functional reference for our research**, not an official NASA module list. Several functions could share one pressurized module. We use “orbital habitat” to include a transit spacecraft operating temporarily in Mars orbit.

## 1. Blocks and their purposes

| Block or system | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Living quarters | Sleeping, meals, hygiene, exercise, and private space during transit and orbital operations. |
| Life support | Maintain breathable air and pressure, remove CO₂, recycle water, manage waste, and control temperature. |
| Command, navigation, and communications | Operate the spacecraft, coordinate docking and surface activities, and communicate with Earth. |
| Science and medical area | Conduct experiments, monitor health, provide treatment, and manage research samples. |
| Workshop and storage | Repair equipment and store food, tools, spares, medical supplies, and consumables. |
| Docking and cargo interfaces | Connect with compatible landers, ascent vehicles, or cargo spacecraft and transfer crew and supplies. |
| Spacewalk access, if required | Allow external inspection or maintenance without depressurizing the occupied habitat. |
| Power and heat control | Generate, store, and distribute electricity and reject waste heat through radiators. |
| Propulsion and attitude control | Maintain orientation and perform the orbital or interplanetary maneuvers required by the vehicle design. |
| Emergency protection and refuge | Detect and suppress fire, provide breathing equipment, isolate damaged spaces, and provide radiation shelter and a survivable refuge. |

Propulsion, radiators, antennas, and power arrays are external systems rather than habitable rooms. Landers and cargo spacecraft are visiting or attached vehicles, not necessarily permanent station modules.

NASA's autonomous-habitat research emphasizes that transit and surface habitats must support crews despite communication delays, limited resources, and long rescue times. [NASA autonomous habitat research](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20220015103)

## 2. Deliveries and return cargo

These are cargo categories for an architecture using orbital rendezvous, not a verified manifest. Surface cargo does not all need to pass through a crewed orbital habitat.

| Route | People or cargo | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Earth → Mars orbit | Crew and their transit habitat; propulsion, power, and communications equipment; food, water and gas reserves, medicines, tools, spares, and experiments | Deliver and support the expedition, including the journey home. |
| Earth → Mars surface separately | Surface habitats or rovers, power systems, instruments, supplies, and ascent equipment | Prepare surface operations without requiring every cargo item to be handled aboard the orbital habitat. |
| Orbital habitat → Mars surface | Astronauts and expedition supplies aboard a lander | Begin surface exploration. |
| Mars surface → orbital habitat | Returning astronauts, selected contained samples, and selected equipment or experiments | Rejoin the return vehicle and prepare people and cargo for Earth return. |
| Mars orbit → Earth | Astronauts, selected samples, research materials, and hardware requiring examination | Complete the expedition and enable analysis on Earth. |

An illustrative sequence is: empty sample containers travel from Earth, astronauts take them to the surface, collect and seal samples, and transfer the contained samples to the return vehicle. This is an example of the route, not an approved mission procedure. Actual Mars sample handling requires appropriate containment and planetary-protection provisions.

Data can be transmitted electronically. Physical cargo requires transport. Local oxygen production on Mars would not automatically supply an orbital habitat: production, storage, ascent transport, and transfer equipment would also be required. MOXIE demonstrated oxygen extraction on the surface, not this supply chain. [NASA: MOXIE mission results](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/mars-2020-perseverance/perseverance-rover/nasas-oxygen-generating-experiment-moxie-completes-mars-mission/)

The original question's **lunar surface → Earth** route is separate. Its principal return categories are astronauts, lunar samples, and selected experiments or hardware; see [lunar Gateway research](lunar-gateway.md). A lunar base supplying a Mars expedition is not an established logistics assumption here.

## 3. Crew and work

There is no assigned operational crew for a NASA Mars orbital station. Crew capacity and occupancy depend on whether the habitat is a transit vehicle, a temporarily occupied staging point, or a separate station.

| Reference or scenario | Crew implication |
|---|---|
| NASA Strategic Analysis Cycle 2021 study | Four people traveled to Mars; two descended and two remained aboard the transport in orbit. This is a historical study assumption. |
| NASA 2025 architecture update | At least four people on the surface, with consideration of up to six. This does not specify a separate resident orbital crew. |
| Proposed separately occupied station | Requires its own crew allocation and support capacity; do not infer that extra crew from the surface number. |

Sources: [NASA SAC21 study](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20220002632) · [NASA 2025 architecture update](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2025-architecture-update-for-publication.pdf).

An orbital crew's work would include spacecraft control, life-support maintenance, scientific experiments, health monitoring, cargo handling, and preparation for surface transfers and Earth return. Crew members would need overlapping operations, engineering, medical, and science skills. These are functional responsibilities, not an official roster of job titles.

If all expedition members descend, the orbital vehicle must support unattended operation for that period. A permanently occupied orbital station should not be assumed necessary for an initial surface expedition.

## 4. Relevance to Flame in Freefall

- Both a transit habitat and a conventional orbital habitat are microgravity environments. Surface habitats experience approximately 0.38g and must be treated separately.
- Ventilation strongly influences oxygen delivery to flames and smoke transport in microgravity. Detection and suppression need to reflect the actual airflow and cabin layout.
- Relevant areas include living quarters, cargo storage, electrical equipment, workshops, and docking interfaces. Their materials and operating conditions differ.
- Oxygen concentration and cabin pressure need their own sourced values. Do not copy an ISS or lunar-habitat atmosphere into the Mars scenario without evidence.
- PSI microgravity investigations can inform prevention, detection, and suppression research, but matching gravity alone does not qualify a system for use. Materials, atmosphere, airflow, and test scale also matter. See our [research brief](../research-brief.md).

## Limits

No final station architecture, orbital parameters, mission duration, cargo masses, or cabin atmosphere were verified. The functional layout is a research assumption. Historical crew splits describe specific studies and should not be presented as current commitments.
