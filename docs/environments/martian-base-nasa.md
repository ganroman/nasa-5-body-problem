# Martian surface base: functions, logistics, and crew

Research reference for **Flame in Freefall** · Team Five-Body Problem · 14 November 2026

A Martian surface base would give astronauts a pressurized place to live, maintain equipment, and conduct surface exploration. Mars has about 38% of Earth's surface gravity. There is currently no inhabited Mars base or assigned operational Mars crew. [NASA: Humans to Mars](https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/humans-to-mars/)

## Status and interpretation

NASA has studied both dedicated surface habitats and pressurized rovers that provide accommodation as well as mobility. These are architectural studies, not a finalized construction plan. The Mars Surface Habitat study explicitly states that it supports design decisions rather than making them. [NASA Mars Surface Habitat concept study](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20230014593)

The breakdown below is a **proposed functional reference for our research**, not an official NASA module list. Several functions could occupy one module. A first expedition need not have a large, permanently occupied settlement.

## 1. Blocks and their purposes

| Block or system | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Living quarters | Sleeping, meals, hygiene, exercise, and private space. |
| Life support | Maintain breathable air and pressure, remove CO₂, recycle water, manage waste, and control temperature. |
| Command and communications | Monitor base systems and coordinate with rovers, orbiting spacecraft, and Earth. |
| Science and sample handling | Examine geology, operate instruments, and contain and document collected samples. |
| Medical area | Monitor crew health and support treatment when evacuation is unavailable. |
| Workshop and storage | Repair equipment and store food, tools, clothing, consumables, and spare parts. |
| Airlock or suit interfaces | Allow surface excursions while limiting atmosphere loss and dust carried indoors. |
| Power and heat control | Generate and distribute electricity, store energy, and manage heating and heat rejection. |
| Emergency protection and refuge | Detect and suppress fires, provide emergency breathing equipment, isolate damaged areas, and provide radiation shelter and a survivable refuge. |
| Surface mobility and science equipment | Rovers, outdoor instruments, and equipment for exploration and handling cargo. |
| Optional local-resource processing | Produce useful consumables from local materials if the necessary equipment and capability have been demonstrated. |
| Ascent and return interface | Provide access to the vehicle that returns the crew to Mars orbit in an architecture using orbital rendezvous. |

Power plants, rovers, and ascent vehicles are separate assets rather than necessarily rooms attached to the habitat. NASA identifies reliable power, shelter, water recycling, food, spacesuits, and communications as key needs for human Mars exploration. [NASA: Humans to Mars](https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/humans-to-mars/)

## 2. Deliveries and return cargo

These are cargo categories, not a verified mission manifest or mass budget.

| Route | People or cargo | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Earth → Mars surface, before crew arrival | Habitats or habitable rovers; power and communications systems; surface tools and instruments; supplies; ascent equipment | Establish the infrastructure. As a proposed operating rule, essential equipment should be checked before the crew depends on it. |
| Earth → Mars surface | Food, medical supplies, clothing, water and atmospheric reserves, suits, filters, tools, and spares | Support the expedition and replace consumables or failed components. Recycling reduces but does not eliminate supply needs. |
| Mars orbit → surface | Astronauts and expedition supplies in a lander | Begin surface operations in an architecture using orbital transfer. Heavy cargo can arrive separately. |
| Mars surface → orbit → Earth | Returning astronauts; selected sealed rock, soil, and atmospheric samples; selected experiments and hardware | Bring people home and support detailed laboratory analysis. Mars sample return requires appropriate containment and planetary-protection provisions. |

Measurements, images, and video can be transmitted electronically; physical samples need a spacecraft. An early expedition should not assume routine emergency resupply from Earth.

### What could eventually be produced locally?

MOXIE demonstrated conversion of atmospheric CO₂ into oxygen on Mars. This was a technology demonstration, not an operational supply plant for a crew. A larger system could produce breathing oxygen and oxidizer for ascent propulsion. Water extraction, fuel manufacture, and substantial food production would each require additional infrastructure and verification. [NASA: MOXIE mission results](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/mars-2020-perseverance/perseverance-rover/nasas-oxygen-generating-experiment-moxie-completes-mars-mission/)

### Clarification of the lunar return route

The original research question also mentioned **lunar surface → Earth**. That is a separate logistics route: returning astronauts, lunar rock and soil samples, and selected experiments or hardware. Moon-produced water, oxygen, or fuel should not be treated as an established supply chain for Earth or Mars. See the separate [lunar Gateway research](lunar-gateway.md).

## 3. Crew and work

NASA's **2025 architecture update** established a planning assumption of **at least four crew members on the Martian surface**, with consideration of up to six. This is not an assigned crew or a final flight manifest. Earlier studies used four expedition members with only two landing; those older assumptions should be identified by date rather than combined with the newer baseline. [NASA 2025 architecture update](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2025-architecture-update-for-publication.pdf)

For a four-person research scenario, the following responsibilities are **proposed**, not an official NASA roster:

| Primary responsibility | Work |
|---|---|
| Commander / vehicle operations | Mission decisions, vehicle operations, and emergency coordination. |
| Habitat / systems engineer | Life support, power, communications, repairs, and maintenance. |
| Science / geology specialist | Field exploration, sampling, scientific instruments, and records. |
| Medical / life-sciences specialist | Health monitoring, treatment, biological research, and environmental monitoring. |

Crew members need overlapping skills. For example, if the engineer is injured, another astronaut must still be able to maintain the air-recycling system. This example illustrates the reason for cross-training; it is not test evidence.

## 4. Relevance to Flame in Freefall

- The occupied interior contains oxygen even though the outside atmosphere is mostly CO₂. Cold weather outside does not prevent an internal fire.
- Mars surface gravity changes buoyancy and smoke movement compared with microgravity. Ventilation remains an important part of the fire environment.
- Oxygen concentration, total cabin pressure, airflow, and materials must be recorded separately. Do not assign a lunar habitat's atmosphere to a Mars base without a source.
- Living areas, storage, workshops, and electrical equipment introduce different materials and ignition sources. Airlocks and suit maintenance introduce dust-management questions.
- Our [research brief](../research-brief.md) identifies no direct partial-gravity combustion data in the PSI collection. Microgravity results can inform research but do not certify a material or suppression system for Mars.

## Limits

Cargo masses, mission duration, cabin atmosphere, detailed power design, and refuge capacity have not been verified. The functional layout and crew responsibilities are research assumptions. A mission-specific safety assessment would require engineering specifications and suitable validation.
