# Schools and public buildings

Environment profile for **Flame in Freefall** · 14 November 2026

Derived from [Earth use cases, Card C](../../earth-use-cases.md#card-c-schools-and-public-buildings). This is a proposed setting for the Cargo Passport, not an assessment of a particular building. Team-input fields and regulatory claims in the source remain subject to review.

A facilities or safety manager would use the passport when furniture, wall panels or devices are brought into, installed or charged in a building. Teachers and parents could read a plain-language account of what the evidence covers and where further evidence is needed.

## Spaces and activities

| Space or stage | What happens | What to distinguish |
|---|---|---|
| Occupied rooms | People use furniture and equipment. | Actual occupancy, item placement, ventilation and available exits. |
| Installed building assemblies | Panels become part of a wall or another system. | Product tested alone versus installed assembly, including mounting and adjacent materials. |
| Device use and charging | Electrical equipment operates or charges. | Powered, charging and stored states, nearby materials and supervision. |

## Operating scenario

The following are explicit modelling assumptions for one representative example. Physical estimates follow in the next table.

| Dimension | Estimated scenario | Basis and limits |
|---|---|---|
| Materials and state | 30 desks/chairs, wall panels and 10 illustrative 50 Wh lithium-ion devices charging at 50% state of charge. | Chosen classroom inventory, not measured fire load; total device nameplate energy 0.5 kWh. |
| Occupancy and access | 30 students and 1 teacher during a lesson; unoccupied overnight. | Selected classroom scenario; assume access to a corridor and onward building exits. |
| Protection and response | Assume smoke detection, audible alarm and portable extinguishers; no automatic sprinklers in the baseline. | Compare a separate sprinkler-equipped scenario if needed; no evacuation or response time is guaranteed. |
| Maintenance and exterior | Annual inspection as a modelling assumption; low-altitude temperate exterior −10 to 35 °C. | Not a prescribed inspection interval or a global climate range. |

## Estimated baseline for comparison

An occupied classroom at a low-altitude site with heating or cooling in operation. A classroom is the initial comparison space; these values do not describe every public building, large assembly hall or exterior wall cavity.

**Evidence status: analyst-selected planning estimates, added at the user’s request.** These are plausible starting inputs, not measurements, validated operating limits or a survey of typical installations. Replace estimates when a specific installation is selected. Gravity is approximately **1g (9.81 m/s²)** in all four baselines.

| Dimension | Estimated value | Basis and limits |
|---|---|---|
| Total gas pressure | 95–103 kPa absolute | Assumed low-altitude room pressure, not a universal building value. |
| Oxygen concentration | 20.9% by volume on a dry-gas basis | Ordinary-air assumption; not a measured indoor gas analysis. |
| Oxygen partial pressure | 19.9–21.5 kPa | Derived: 95–103 × 0.209, rounded; dry-gas approximation. |
| Local airflow | 0.05–0.2 m/s in the occupied zone | Chosen low-draft comparison range; near-window and supply-jet speeds can be higher. |
| Air temperature | 18–27 °C | Assumed conditioned occupied-room range; seasonal excursions need separate scenarios. |
| Relative humidity | 30–60% | Assumed indoor range, not a claim that every school controls humidity. |
| Space dimensions and volume | Classroom: 8 × 7 × 3 m = 168 m³ | Illustrative room before subtracting furniture and occupants; wall cavities require their own geometry. |

For this ordinary-air scenario, approximate the remaining dry gas as 78% N₂, 0.9% argon and trace gases. Humidity, CO₂ accumulation and fault gases require separate inputs. Local speed is not an air-change rate: do not infer room ventilation performance from these ranges.

## Evidence transfer and limits

**Transfer: method plus potentially relevant smoke-detection data.** SAME, SAME-R and PSI-115 concern smoke detection, but their test conditions still need comparison with the room, airflow and smoke source. BASS/Saffire material results do not directly establish the performance of building products or whole assemblies in 1g.

Card C identifies EU construction-product fire classifications and Declarations of Performance, and US NFPA 101, as existing records and frameworks to connect to. Applicable jurisdiction, current legal requirements, product scope and adopted editions need checking. A product classification is not a finding that every installation containing it is safe.

**Illustrative comparison:** a manager receives a panel with a fire classification, then checks whether the supporting test used the same backing, mounting and joints as the proposed wall. Differences stay visible for professional assessment. This example is not test evidence.

The passport organises evidence; it does not replace building codes, a fire engineer's assessment or checks that submitted evidence is authentic.
