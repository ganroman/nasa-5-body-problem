# Recording summary: packaging, hooks and mentor feedback (14 Nov, afternoon)

Full transcript: `Raw - Packaging, Hooks and Mentor Feedback.md`. This summary was written with SI assistance (Claude); it only restates the recording and adds fact checks.

## What was discussed

**1. Framework: material × process × environment**
- **Environments:** launch, outer space, the Moon, the space station.
- **Processes** (what astronauts do): entering and exiting, changing equipment inside or outside, docking, moving between spacecraft.
- **Materials** that can catch fire. Which materials you meet depends on the situation (the paper-in-the-toilet vs forest comparison).
- **Two jobs to teach:** prevention, the focus so far, and extinguishing/mitigation, newly added ("strict rules what to do in case of one, two, three").

**2. New angle: a system with moving parts.** A mission like Artemis II is closed: nothing comes in or goes out. Future bases aren't. People move between spacecraft and bases run by different agencies with different standards, and they bring materials that the receiving side may never have tested. Someone compared it to customs.

**3. Ignition sources named:** heating from the sun, battery discharge, engine heat carried inside. In microgravity, heat stays where it is unless air moves, and "you cannot open the window."

**4. Materials tested only on Earth.** The team found MAPTIS (NASA's material flammability database). The idea is to "pre-evaluate" how Earth-tested materials might behave in microgravity or on the Moon, and rank them. The example given was lithium in batteries: how much is safe to carry, and where to store it. The team asked "ranking based on what?" and left it open.

**5. Agencies handle fire differently.** One claim: on the ISS, US crew carry CO₂ extinguishers and Russian crew carry water-based ones. The proposal is a shared prevention and extinguishing approach, with Earth uses in planes, buildings and batteries. How the Chinese station handles fire is unknown and worth checking. Also noted, credited to "super intelligence" in the recording: extinguishing makes the air less breathable, since CO₂ displaces oxygen. Different extinguisher types for electronics zones vs living areas were mentioned too.

**6. Three hook options were read out**
1. **Material passport:** "This material is going to the Moon. Does its evidence travel with it?"
2. **Find the boundary:** "How far can you change the cabin before you leave tested territory?" Adjust oxygen, pressure and airflow; real experiments appear on a map; untested combinations become visible; a report shows the nearest measurements and the next validation question.
3. **Build the next test:** design the next experiment. The team called it "not very striking."

The team leaned toward **combining 1 and 2**: a material check plus situational awareness of "what happens if you do something". It's aimed at prevention and at an "average Joe", not only scientists.

- Around **40 presentations**. Be memorable: a small video, a gimmick or gamification.
- Keep the first part simple; technical depth comes out in the questions.
- Choose the **one or two best presenters**.

## Fact checks and open points

1. **Where the hooks came from.** The recording says "I cannot bring us ideas from [Claude] but Codex can", and the three hooks sound like they were read from a list. If an AI tool wrote them, that conflicts with the rule *"SI should not be used to (come up with your idea of how to solve the challenge)."* Record who came up with which hook, and disclose any SI involvement.
2. **ISS extinguishers.** The Researcher's Guide (2015) says water-mist extinguishers began replacing CO₂ units on the ISS in December 2015. Verify the current state before pitching the "US carries CO₂" contrast.
3. **Ranking untested materials.** Without a published, validated model, a ranking or coefficient is a guess. Keep it qualitative unless a model is verified. Check whether MAPTIS data is publicly accessible; parts of it may need an account.
4. **Batteries and lithium.** There's no battery fire data in PSI, so this example needs another source.
5. **Two pitch formats.** Locally there's a ~2-minute live pitch, in addition to the 30-second video for global judging. Plan both, and pick the presenter.
