A story from the library

What on Earth uses space-fire research?

Fire research on the station is meant to help life on the ground too. This story follows the few links the sources state, and marks that none of them names a product.

Partly data

Each observation comes from a NASA page, guide or paper. A product or a standard that changed because of this research is not covered by the sources we read.

01 of 04

A flame that dies on Earth, lives on the station

Cool flames have been produced on Earth, but they quickly flicker out.1 On the station, cool flames can burn for minutes, giving scientists a better opportunity to study them.1

02 of 04

Why that matters on the ground

The principal investigator of the CFI-G gas-flame experiment says cool flames are important to study because engine technology is trending toward lower temperatures.2 Little is known about combustion chemistry at these temperatures, and experiments like CFI-G could help.2

03 of 04

A test written for Earth

NASA's researcher guide says recent results from the BASS experiment indicate that the NASA-STD-6001 Test 1 screening test is not conservative.3 Materials can burn at lower oxygen levels in microgravity than on Earth.3

04 of 04

What we cannot yet say

A product on Earth that came from this research is not covered by the sources we read.

What this story does not show

  • The engine link is the opinion of the principal investigator of CFI-G.
  • The cool-flame statement gives no number for how quickly the flames flicker out.
  • No sentence here is a verdict on any material, engine or item.
