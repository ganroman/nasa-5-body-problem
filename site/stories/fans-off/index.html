Does switching off the fans put fire out?

On the station an alarm switches the fans off to starve a fire. This story follows the tests where the air was cut, and marks that the question has no answer yet for a fire in a crewed cabin.

Partly data

Each observation comes from a NASA paper or a flight test, but they cover different fuels and none burned in a crewed cabin, so no single answer follows from them.

01 of 05

The alarm switches the fans off

When the ISS fire alarm sounds, intramodular and intermodular fans are deactivated, and gas introduction and oxygen generation are stopped.1 The aim is to stop air and oxygen flowing toward the fire, so that it can burn itself out.1 NASA's fire-protection brief lists the response as terminating ventilation in the affected module to slow the spread of fire.2

02 of 05

Some flames go out

In the Mir low-speed wind tunnel, increasing the flow rate made the flames strengthen significantly, and when the forced air flow was stopped the flames self-extinguished.3 In Saffire-I a large fabric flame burned for about 7 minutes and went out immediately when the air flow was shut off.4 In one BASS-II test on a 100-micrometre PMMA film, the flame went out within 2 seconds of the flow being cut off.5

03 of 05

Some flames wait

In a Saffire V test on thin cotton fabric, the fan was off for 70 seconds, and the flame grew back when the flow returned.6 The Saffire results caution that putting out thin charring fuels at increased oxygen cannot be assumed to happen rapidly in quiescent conditions.7 NASA's researcher guide lists as critical misconceptions the beliefs that a steady fire cannot exist in still microgravity air and that a fire will self-extinguish if ventilation is shut off.8

04 of 05

On the Moon the fans do not decide

A NASA slide says that turning the ECLSS flow off to extinguish a fire in lunar gravity is not effective, because reduced gravity will still induce buoyant flow velocities.9

05 of 05

What we cannot yet say

No source we read measures switching the fans off in a fire in a crewed cabin. Which of the two outcomes, out or waiting, a cargo fire would take is not covered by the sources we read.

What this story does not show

  • The alarm response is a design intent, not a measured outcome.
  • No result here comes from a fire in a crewed cabin.
  • No test here covers the fuels of a cargo item.
  • The figure is a simplified drawing of a flame in a duct, not a recording of the flight.
  • No sentence here is a verdict on any material or item.
