A story from the library

How do you put out a weightless fire?

When nothing lifts a flame away, what makes it go out? This story follows tests that cut the air, left it still, or let it come back, and marks where the answers stop.

Data

Each observation comes from a flight experiment or a NASA paper on it. They are separate tests on different fuels, so the common reading is ours.

01 of 06

No up to carry the flame away

On a spacecraft, ventilation fans replace natural convection and can supply the air a fire needs to burn.1 So what happens when the air stops?

02 of 06

Cut the flow, a thin film goes out

In the BASS-II experiments a flame spread across half of a 100-micrometre PMMA film at a steady rate.2 When the flow was abruptly cut off, the flame was observed to extinguish within 2 seconds.2

03 of 06

Cut the flow, thin cotton comes back

In a Saffire V test on thin cotton fabric, the fan was off for 70 seconds, and the flame grew back when the flow was turned on again.3 It burned for another 150 seconds until the material was consumed.3 Both flamelets and embers were present when imaging stopped, so the authors cannot tell whether the flame survived the next 60 seconds.4

04 of 06

Still air is not a switch

The Saffire results caution that putting out thin charring fuels at increased oxygen cannot be assumed to happen rapidly in quiescent conditions.5 Experiments have shown that even a very slight air flow of a fraction of a cm/s is sufficient to allow the flame to survive.6 These flames can become almost undetectable, small and non-luminous, and yet persist for many minutes.6

05 of 06

A flame can go out and the fire stay

A fuel droplet on the space station appeared to extinguish but continued to burn without a visible flame.7

06 of 06

What we cannot yet say

No source we read tests putting out a fire in a loaded cargo bag.

What this story does not show

  • The BASS-II result is one described test on a very thin film.
  • The cotton result is one test on one thin fabric, and the cause of the regrowth is a judgement of the authors.
  • No test here covers droplets, films and fabrics together.
  • The figure is a simplified drawing of a flame in a duct, not a recording of the flight.
  • No sentence here is a verdict on any material or item.
