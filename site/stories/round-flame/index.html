Why is a flame round in space?

A flame on Earth leans up and flickers. This story follows what the sources say makes it so, what a flame looks like when that pull is gone, and where the answers stop.

Data

Each observation comes from a NASA document or a flight experiment on a flame. How a candle shape maps to the shape of other fires is not covered by the sources we read.

01 of 05

The flame we know

A flame on Earth makes an eight-fold density change over the scale of a centimetre.1 This density gradient induces a strong velocity field that causes the flame to lift and point upward.1 The resulting flow instabilities cause the flicker typical of flames on Earth.1

02 of 05

The pull that is gone

NASA's fire-protection brief says that as hot gases from the flame rise, gravity pulls cooler, denser air to the bottom of the flame, which regulates its speed and size.2 The same brief says this also creates the shape of the flame and its flickering, and that in microgravity this flow does not occur.2

03 of 05

A candle with no up

A candle lit in weightlessness aboard the Space Shuttle was spherical and bright yellow just after ignition.3 After 8 to 10 seconds the yellow, presumably from soot, disappeared, and the flame became blue and nearly hemispherical.3

On the Mir station a candle flame in weightlessness was nearly invisible to the naked eye, and a very long shutter time was needed to record its image.4 NASA's fire-protection brief says that flames in nearly still or low-oxygen air are often pale blue and almost invisible.5

04 of 05

Add a draught and the shape changes

On a spacecraft, ventilation fans replace natural convection and can supply the air a fire needs to burn.6 Under these circumstances the fire can spread in any direction, rather than just upward.6

05 of 05

What we cannot yet say

What shape a fire takes in a loaded cargo bag is not covered by the sources we read.

What this story does not show

  • Candle results are not results for cargo.
  • The soot explanation for the yellow glow is the authors' presumption.
  • The figure is a simplified drawing of a flame in a duct, not a recording of the flight.
  • No sentence here is a verdict on any material or item.
