What does a battery fire do in orbit?

We found no report of a battery fire in orbit, so this story gives only what the handbooks say about the power needed to start a fire, and where batteries are kept.

Hypothesis

No direct measurement of a battery fire in weightlessness is in our sources. What follows is what the safety handbooks and requirements say, not what a battery does.

01 of 06

No battery fire reported yet

None of the sources we read reports a battery fire in orbit. What they do hold is rules: how much electrical power is thought enough to start a fire, and where powered items may sit.

02 of 06

Power decides

For flammability considerations, any high-power electrical wire is a potential ignition source.1 Any item that could cause sparks, such as a brush motor, is also considered a potential ignition source.1

Small, low-voltage alkaline and coin batteries are still believed to be incapable of delivering powers required for ignition.2 Larger batteries, such as lithium-ion rechargeable batteries for portable electronic devices, can certainly achieve higher power levels.2

03 of 06

The number is not settled

The handbook says a reevaluation of an earlier test found that the actual voltage was considerably lower than reported and the power was more like 5 watts.3 It adds that further testing is required before a new power threshold can be identified.3

04 of 06

Less gravity, less power

Combustion theory and very limited experimental data indicate that the electrical power required for ignition may be lower in partial gravity than in Earth gravity.4 In the absence of specific data, the handbook advises a maximum short-circuit power draw of less than 2 watts for lunar and Martian environments.4 That advice is about the Moon and Mars, not about orbit.

05 of 06

Where batteries are kept

The ISS and MPCV Programs allow fully certified and undamaged batteries, including lithium-ion rechargeable batteries, to be stowed inside stowage bags without additional containment.5 Flammable materials stowed in lockers do not constitute a fire risk while in the containers, provided they are unpowered.6

Powered payloads stowed in lockers for an entire mission are to be evaluated from a flammability standpoint because they present a potential fire hazard.6 When deployed in the open cabin for use, flammable items will be kept away from rack power outlets, utility outlet panels and power strips.7

06 of 06

What we cannot yet say

How a failing battery behaves in weightlessness, with its heat, smoke and gases, is not covered by the sources we read.

What this story does not show

  • No test of a battery in weightlessness is in our sources.
  • The rules here are handbook guidance for ISS and partial-gravity design, not measurements of a battery.
  • The 2 watts is guidance used in the absence of specific data, and the handbook says a new power threshold is not yet identified.
  • No sentence here is a verdict on any battery or item.
