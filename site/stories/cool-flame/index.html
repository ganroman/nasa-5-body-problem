A story from the library

What is a cool flame?

On the space station a fire went out twice: once with a visible flame and once without. This story follows that second stage and marks where the answers stop.

Data

Each observation comes from a NASA page or a paper on droplet and gas-flame experiments. None of the sources we read reports a cool flame on a solid material.

01 of 05

A fire that went out twice

On the space station a fuel droplet appeared to extinguish but actually continued to burn without a visible flame.1 NASA says the fire went out twice, once with and once without a visible flame.1

02 of 05

The second stage

Scientists observed for the first time large droplets of heptane fuel with dual modes of combustion and extinction.2 The second stage was sustained by what is known as cool flame chemical heat release.2 Non-premixed cool flames, made when fuel and oxidizer are not mixed before reacting, were discovered in 2012 aboard the space station during the Flame Extinguishment (FLEX) studies.3

03 of 05

Cameras that see nothing

In one n-decane droplet test at 21 percent oxygen and 0.5 atm, the colour and ultraviolet cameras were not sensitive enough to register any flame image during the cool-flame stage.4 Radiometers still recorded continued radiative heat loss from the droplet, which the authors read as active chemical heat release.4

04 of 05

Why anyone studies it

The principal investigator of the CFI-G gas-flame experiment says cool flames are important to study because engine technology is trending toward lower temperatures.5 Little is known about combustion chemistry at these temperatures, and experiments like CFI-G could help.5

05 of 05

What we cannot yet say

In the Combustion Integrated Rack the first five studies burned liquid droplets, the next six burned gaseous fuels, and the current series studies solid fuels such as PMMA.6 Among the sources we checked, none reports a cool flame on a solid material or a cargo item.6

What this story does not show

  • Droplet and gas-flame results are not results for a solid material or a cargo item.
  • That the cool stage is "active chemical heat release" is the authors' reading of radiometer data.
  • The NASA summary page gives no oxygen level, pressure or droplet size for the heptane result.
  • No sentence here is a verdict on any material or item.
