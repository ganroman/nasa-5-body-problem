01 / 0:00–0:10 / 10 seconds

Start with the passport icebreaker.

Suggested presentation

“Do you have a passport?” [Pause for yes.]
“Do you show it when travelling?” [Pause for yes.]
“So your cargo needs a passport when travelling in space.”

What happens · what to show

Ask the audience the first question, pause briefly, then ask the second. After their replies, point to the cargo and deliver the final line. The passport is our evidence-organizing metaphor, not a claim that a literal cargo passport is legally required. The ISS → lunar habitat journey remains hypothetical.

Keep each audience pause to about one second. If nobody answers, smile and continue. Rehearse the whole exchange within ten seconds; team identification stays on screen.

Q&A / 0:50–1:00 / 10 seconds

One brief question, if time allows.

Suggested presentation

Allow roughly five seconds for a short question and five for a one-sentence answer. This is a minimal question window, not a full discussion. Stop at 1:00.

Ready answers

“No. It highlights evidence gaps; approval stays with the authority.”

What does AI do?
“Source-grounded explanations are planned; the mock has no live AI.”

What works today?

Delivery checklist

Make the minute feel focused.

  • One speaker, one teammate controlling slides and playback.
  • Rehearse to 0:50 before questions and 1:00 overall; transitions count.
  • Use the same cargo and component throughout.
  • Embed media locally and check sound and accurate captions.
  • Keep a product screenshot ready if playback fails.
  • Remove risk scores and unverified prediction claims from the video.
Evidence & scope

Keep claims matched to the build.

This plan follows the cargo build plan, the video review, and the official judging criteria: Impact, Creativity, Validity, Relevance and Presentation.

The repository prototype explicitly uses dummy data. Neither these slide mockups nor the original video establish validated fire prediction, cargo approval, or an integrated AI feature.

The selected ISS → lunar habitat journey is hypothetical, as recorded in the route evidence review (R23). It is not a reported recent flight. The 60-second local pitch is separate from the global submission demo format: a video up to 30 seconds or a deck up to seven slides.
