# Review comments: brag.mp4

Reviewed: 15 November 2026. Target: [brag.mp4](brag.mp4), not `cargo-journey.mp4`.

The cargo-passport story is worth keeping. Change the risk ratings and prediction claims so the jury sees what the product can support: relevant experimental evidence, differences in conditions, and questions that still need testing.

## Observed wording and suggested changes

Times below identify sampled frames, not exact scene boundaries.

| Sample time | Observed wording or visual | Why it matters | Suggested change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 s | “See every fire risk inside.” | “Every” implies comprehensive hazard detection that the repository does not establish. | “See the evidence behind your cargo.” |
| 0 s | HIGH cargo fire risk; HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW ratings for materials | The ratings look like scientific conclusions. The current dashboard uses dummy data, and the build plan does not establish a validated safety-rating method. | Show “Relevant evidence”, “Conditions differ”, or “Evidence missing”, supported by the actual comparison. These describe evidence status, not danger levels. |
| 0 s | “Higher O₂ than ISS, stronger flames” | This presents a general conclusion for the selected cargo without showing material-specific support or accounting for the other changed conditions. | “Oxygen conditions differ from the cited tests.” Use this only when the displayed source comparison establishes the difference. |
| 5 and 10 s | “Every shipment gets a passport.” | The passport is a clear, memorable product metaphor. “Every shipment” describes the ambition rather than demonstrated coverage. | Keep the metaphor; consider “An evidence passport for your cargo.” |
| 20, 25 and 29 s | “Predict the fire. Plan the response.” | This implies a validated predictive model and operational response guidance. Neither is established in the reviewed repository. | “Compare the conditions. Identify the next test.” |
| 20, 25 and 29 s | Flame-spread panel labelled “preliminary model”; “FLAMES BURN BLUE” | A model label can imply implemented scientific prediction. The blanket flame-colour statement also needs qualification and supporting evidence. | Replace this panel with a cited finding, its measured conditions, and the unresolved validation question. Show a prediction only if its model, applicability and checks can be demonstrated. |
| Multiple frames | Small “CONCEPT · ILLUSTRATIVE DATA” footer | The disclosure helps, but prominent risk and prediction claims still dominate the viewer's interpretation. | Keep a readable disclosure and align the main headlines and visuals with it. |

## Suggested 30-second story

This is a proposed edit, not a description of the current video or proof of implemented features.

| Time | Show | Main message |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 s | One science instrument travelling from transit to a lunar habitat | “The cargo travels. Does its evidence?” |
| 5–12 s | Its passport, one component, and a source-linked experiment | “See what NASA tested.” |
| 12–22 s | Change the destination; show condition differences while experimental points remain fixed | “See what changes—and what remains unknown.” |
| 22–27 s | Relevant evidence and one specific unanswered question | “Identify what still needs testing.” |
| 27–30 s | The scenario passport and product/team identity | “Take the evidence with you.” |

Use verified source content where available. If the sequence still uses illustrative data, identify it clearly as a concept demonstration. Do not imply that a component test establishes safety of the assembled cargo.

## Basis and review limits

- The inspected video is exactly **30.0 seconds** long and contains an audio track.
- Visual review sampled frames at **0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 and 29 seconds**. It was not a continuous playback review; audio was not reviewed.
- The [final build plan](../docs/build-plan.md) describes evidence comparison and explicitly excludes automatic approval, safety scores and unsupported predictions. It specifies intended work, not completed implementation.
- The [dashboard mock](../prototype/dashboard-mock.html) labels its records as dummy data and states that no live SI or prediction model is connected.
- The [judging guide](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/judging-awards-guide/#judging-criteria) evaluates scientific validity, meaningful NASA data integration and presentation, alongside impact and creativity. Clear separation of demonstrated behavior and planned capabilities helps the jury assess those criteria.

No video changes were made. The main remaining risk is that viewers interpret illustrative ratings or planned prediction features as validated results.
