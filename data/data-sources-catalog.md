# Data sources for Flame in Freefall

Compiled 14 November 2026 from project records and the research discussed with the team. This catalogue contains source descriptions and links, not experimental data. It includes databases, published experimental reports and supporting references; a report or facility page is not necessarily a downloadable dataset.

**Relevance:** **Core** directly supports interpreting spacecraft combustion experiments; **High** supports the proposed freefall-to-space comparison or material selection; **Conditional** supports an optional battery or modelling extension; **Context** explains methods or scope.

The proposed research direction is to use Earth-based freefall experiments to develop predictions for specified fuel, geometry, oxygen, pressure and airflow, then evaluate them against independent space experiments. This remains a hypothesis, not a validated capability. Inclusion here does not mean a source appears in the official challenge Resources tab: that list could not be verified during our research.

## 2. Earth-based freefall experiments

These sources establish related experiment families. We have not yet verified individual ground/space runs with matching conditions. Only the ZARM entry below has been identified in this research as a public downloadable ground dataset; its files have not yet been inspected.

| Source and link | Description | Relevance to our challenge | Access and interpretation limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| [NASA BASS documentation: PMMA sphere drop-tower experiments](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/burning-and-suppression-of-solids-bass.pdf?emrc=3a5c2c) | Describes acrylic-plastic sphere burning, controlled airflow and nitrogen suppression in ground microgravity tests. | **High:** direct programme connection to ISS BASS; candidate for solid-fuel response and suppression comparisons. | Public report; reusable raw ground records still need to be located. |
| [SSCE hardware description and ground-based test results](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19890010075/downloads/19890010075.pdf?attachment=true) | Drop-tower ignition and sample-preparation tests supporting the Shuttle Solid Surface Combustion Experiment. | **High:** direct ground-to-flight connection for solid-fuel flame spread and ignition. | Public report. | [Princeton/NASA: Science Support for Space-Based Droplet Combustion](https://www.princeton.edu/~fldryer/nasa.dir/fldryer.pdf) | Research combining NASA drop-tower studies, modelling and Shuttle droplet experiments. | **High:** direct connection to FSDC/DCE; related physics to FLEX and FLEX-2. | Public research report; raw records and exact condition matches remain to be checked. |
| [Princeton Fuels and Combustion Research Laboratory: drop-tower experiments](https://www.princeton.edu/~combust/research/mgc/experiments/droptower.html) | Overview of droplet combustion experiments and diagnostics used in NASA drop towers. | **High:** helps locate ground work and understand droplet imaging and experimental methods. | Research overview, not a confirmed bulk dataset. |
| [NASA: Candle Flames in Microgravity](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19960008390/downloads/19960008390.pdf) | Compares candle research across drop towers, aircraft and Shuttle experiments. | **High for comparison methods:** a clear demonstration of the difference between short and long microgravity observations. | Public paper. Candle behaviour does not represent every fuel or geometry. |
| [ZARM: Flame Spread and Heat Transfer over Discrete Fuels separated by Metal Barriers under Microgravity](https://zenodo.org/records/18479713) | Public dataset containing infrared recordings of thin PMMA fuel strips in Bremen drop-tower experiments, with a conditions index. | **High:** strongest open ground-data candidate identified for solid-fuel flame spread, geometry and airflow comparisons. | Downloadable dataset identified, not yet validated locally. Related to BASS-II/SoFIE questions, not a verified paired space campaign. |
| [NASA reduced-gravity drop towers and test rigs](https://www.nasa.gov/glenn/glenn-expertise-space-exploration/physical-sciences-program/reduced-gravity-drop-towers-and-test-rigs/) | Facility descriptions and capabilities for Earth-based microgravity research. | **Context:** explains achievable observation windows and experimental constraints. | Facility reference, not experimental data. |

## 4. Battery failure as an optional ignition-source extension

These sources concern induced battery failures and their consequences. They do not, by themselves, establish mission failure probabilities, orbital battery-fire behaviour or ignition of nearby materials.

| Source and link | Description | Relevance to our challenge | Access and interpretation limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Battery Failure Databank](https://nrel.sitefinity.cloud/transportation/battery-failure) | Ground battery-abuse test collection with downloadable records of thermal-runaway consequences and associated imaging for some tests. | **Conditional:** strongest reusable-data candidate identified for a battery-failure consequence explorer. | Download and records not yet validated locally. Match exact cell models, state of charge and trigger methods; cell format alone does not establish flight use. |
| [NASA GS Yuasa LSE-134 thermal-runaway calorimetry study](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/characterization-of-the-gs-yuasa-134-cell-thermal-runaway-events-with-large-format-fractional-thermal-runaway-calorimetry.pdf?emrc=6993d3513fc69) | Ground calorimetry research on the large-format cell model used in ISS main batteries. | **Conditional:** direct connection to spacecraft battery hardware and measured failure consequences. | Public presentation; reusable raw measurement availability remains unverified. Ground tests do not establish microgravity transfer. |
| [NASA ISS Main Battery Thermal Runaway Propagation Test](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20180000927) | Research on thermal-runaway propagation in an ISS battery assembly. | **Conditional:** assembly-level evidence for propagation and containment. | Published report; isolated-cell outcomes should not be substituted for pack-level behaviour. |
| [ICES-2025-426: battery heat release, aerosol and gas products](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20250005133/downloads/ICES_Fina_426.pdf) | Initial tests of battery cells in a ventilated enclosure, measuring thermal and airborne consequences relevant to spacecraft cabins. | **Conditional:** connects battery failures to smoke, gases and cabin hazards. | Public paper; complete device and battery-pack experiments described as future work are not reported results. |

## 5. Fire mitigation, response and recovery

These sources support the proposed mitigation evidence explorer: explain how fire response limits damage after ignition, then connect the principles to suppression experiments. They are published guidance, design requirements and research, not verified current vehicle-specific crew checklists.

| Source and link | Description | Relevance to our challenge | Access and interpretation limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| [NASA Fire Protection Technical Brief, OCHMO-TB-008](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/ochmo-tb-008-fire-protection.pdf) | Public summary of spacecraft fire protection. Page 3 describes the ISS strategy, covering detection, ventilation control, breathing protection, electrical isolation, suppression and post-fire atmospheric recovery. | **High for mitigation:** strongest starting reference for explaining response principles and why extinguishing a flame does not end the emergency. | Reference brief derived from NASA-STD-3001; it explicitly does not replace programme requirements. The reviewed revision is dated 29 November 2023. Do not present its equipment descriptions as a verified current inventory or its summary as an operational checklist. |
| [NASA-STD-3001 Volume 2, §9.8.2: Fire Protection System](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/9-0-hardware-and-equipment-vol-2/) | Requirements for spacecraft fire detection, warning and extinguishing systems, including system status and crew access considerations. | **High for mitigation design:** connects response capabilities to requirements for protecting crew and spacecraft. | Public requirements reference, not step-by-step emergency instructions. Preserve the applicable standard revision when citing a requirement. |
| [Development of the International Space Station Fine Water Mist Portable Fire Extinguisher](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20130011664) | NASA development research explaining water-mist suppression and the breathing-environment concerns associated with CO₂ discharge and filtering respirators. | **High for suppression:** supports comparing suppressant effects on both fire and crew exposure, alongside BASS and FLEX evidence. | Public NTRS record for a 2013 development paper. Historical development findings do not establish current equipment deployment or a universal choice of suppressant for every fire. |
| [Fire Response Aboard the International Space Station](https://saemobilus.sae.org/papers/fire-response-aboard-international-space-station-972334) | NASA-authored paper describing fire response, cleanup, procedure validation and training for the early ISS U.S. segment. | **Context for mitigation:** useful background on linking response actions, post-fire recovery and training scenarios. | Historical 1997 paper with scope through assembly mission 7A. Public abstract identified; full-text access was not verified. Not evidence of current flight procedures. |

Use these references to explain response principles, with source dates and scope visible. Link to BASS/FLEX for experimental suppression evidence and SAME for smoke measurements; experiments do not independently authorize crew actions. A mitigation scenario should distinguish flame suppression, continuing or renewed burning, atmospheric contamination and recovery, and should identify where evidence is missing rather than generate operational instructions.

## 6. ISS cargo and payload acceptance requirements

These references explain how fire hazards are assessed before cargo or equipment is accepted for flight. They are requirements and guidance, not experimental datasets or a complete cargo approval checklist. Applicability depends on the item, its configuration, intended use, ISS segment and transport vehicle. Confirm controlled document revisions with the responsible integration and safety teams.

| Source and link | Description | Relevance to our challenge | Access and interpretation limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| [NASA ISS Payload Design Guide](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/pdguide-complete-20240927.pdf) | Explains payload development and phased safety reviews, from early design through final acceptance, including documentation of hazards, controls and verification. | **High for prevention:** provides the review context for a tool that helps designers locate fire-safety evidence and identify gaps. | Public September 2024 guide; an orientation resource, not the complete set of controlling requirements for a particular shipment. |
| [ISS Safety Requirements Document, SSP 51721](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20210009936) | Requirements covering ISS end items, including hardware, payloads and logistics. Includes material flammability, acceptable control strategies and toxic offgassing in habitable areas. | **High for prevention:** links material selection and whole-item configuration to containment, stowage, use restrictions and verification. | Public baseline edition identified; do not assume it is the latest controlled revision. Some combustible items may be acceptable with reviewed controls; a material name or MAPTIS entry alone does not establish flight approval. |
| [Crewed Space Vehicle Battery Safety Requirements, JSC 20793 Rev D](https://standards.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/standards/JSC/D/0/jsc-20793_revd.pdf) | Battery hazard controls, design evaluation and verification for vehicle, payload and crew-equipment batteries before certification. | **High for battery prevention:** complements failure experiments with the requirements used to assess a battery-powered item. | Public 2017 revision identified; confirm the applicable controlled version. Requirements depend on the battery design and mission; consumer-product approval does not establish ISS acceptance. |
| [NASA Hazardous Material Summary Tables (HMSTs)](https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/esdmd/hhp/hazardous-material-summary-tables-hmsts/) | Explains chemical and biological material submissions and hazard assessment, including toxicity, biosafety, flammability, labelling and operational response. Directs ISS submissions through the electronic HMST system. | **High for cargo hazards:** connects the contents of a shipment to containment, hazard communication and response information. | Public process guidance; electronic submissions require access. Check the referenced definitions to determine which materials require submission. This is not a public catalogue of approved cargo. |
| [NASA Quick Start Guide to Payload Design](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/quick-start-guide-to-payload-design/) | Entry point for payload design guidance covering materials, electrical and thermal considerations and certification support. | **Context for integration:** helps designers understand how fire prevention fits the wider station integration process. | Public overview; use the assigned integration process to establish exact requirements. |
| [ISS Pressurized Payloads Interface Requirements Document, SSP 57000 Rev R](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20190001390) | Interface design requirements and guidance for payloads within its stated pressurized-ISS scope. | **High for equipment integration:** connects payload configuration and station interfaces to safe installation and operation. | Public 2015 revision identified; scope varies by partner and module. Confirm the applicable revision and vehicle-specific requirements rather than treating this as a universal cargo rulebook. |

Use these sources alongside MAPTIS and NASA-STD-6001 in section 3. A proposed designer-facing feature could map an item to relevant requirements and supporting experiments, showing missing evidence. It should distinguish material test results from approval of the assembled item and its operations; final acceptance belongs to the responsible safety review process.

## Use in the project

Start with PSI plus one comparable ground experiment family. Add MAPTIS or public material studies when exact material identity and test conditions can be matched. Keep the battery extension optional until its data and its connection to surrounding-material ignition are established.

For each source used in the app, preserve the material identity, specimen geometry, oxygen, pressure, airflow, ignition method, gravity environment, observation duration and source reference where available. Missing conditions stay unknown. Distinguish observed results from model predictions and extrapolations. A short-drop flame surviving does not establish sustained burning in space, and orbital validation does not validate lunar or Martian gravity.

Remaining gaps include verified matched ground/space runs, long-duration transfer, partial-gravity coverage for the selected scenarios, and reusable battery or electrical-fault evidence under space conditions. External lunar/Martian temperature, activity and live-sensor sources were discussed as possible extensions but no specific suitable datasets were verified; they are not presented here as available combustion evidence.
